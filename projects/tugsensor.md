The goal of this project was to design and build a functional prototype of a Tactical Unattended Ground (TUG) sensor. These type of sensors are covert, remotely deployed surveillance devices designed to detect, track, and report situational activity, such as movement of seismic disturbances, in remote or hazardous environments without human presence. The system was engineered to fit within a standard tennis ball, creating a compact form suitable for drone deployment.

As an Electrical Engineering Intern, I drove the end-to-end prototyping process from component selection to field testing. I selected and integrated core hardware (e.g. microcontroller, vibration sensor, motion tracking sensor) and designed the circuit to run autonomously on battery power. After assembling and validating the circuit on a breadboard, I conducted outdoor field evaluations to calibrate motion-tracking range and determine critical vibration alert thresholds. Additionally, I built a custom GUI to visualize real-time feedback and trigger alerts if sensor readings passed a certain threshold.

This project provided hands on experience in embedded systems engineering, low-power circuit design, and autonomous sensor telemetry. Working within extreme spatial and power constraints showcased the importance of component trade-offs when scaling tactical electronics. I would love to explore more methods and sensor to expand the capabilities of my TUG sensor such as integrating acoustic microphones for noise detection or infrared cameras for thermal verification.
